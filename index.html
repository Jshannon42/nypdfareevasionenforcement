Who gets penalized for evading NYC’s transit fare?

Black and Hispanic New Yorkers made up approximately 92% of the total people arrested for fare evasion from April 2026 to June 2026, according to recent police data.

By Jada Shannon

Published on November 25, 2026


Recent data from the New York City Police Department reveals a racial disparity in fare evasion enforcement. Black New Yorkers make up about 20% of the population, but they were approximately 64% of the people arrested by the NYPD for evading the $3.00 transit fare. Hispanic New Yorkers were about 29% of total arrests. White New Yorkers were 5% of arrests, though they were 18% of people summoned for not paying the fare. Across backgrounds, the majority of people arrested were men, who made up 74% of summonses and 95% of arrests.



Police reports reveal that most arrests came from highly trafficked train stations in predominantly Black and brown neighborhoods, such as: Nostrand Avenue in Crown Heights, Myrtle Avenue in Bed-Stuy, Livonia Avenue in Brownsville, and Broadway in East New York. Central hubs for transferring between trains also topped the list, such as Atlantic Avenue in Downtown Brooklyn and Parsons/Archer-Jamaica Center in Queens. Summonses data suggests that people are more likely to receive a summons instead of an arrest at train stations in Manhattan, with most summonses occurring 42 Street-Port Authority, Grand Central, and Times Square.

Methodology

Each quarter, the New York City Police Department releases fare evasion enforcement data based on arrests and summonses made in the previous quarter. They provide a breakdown by race, gender, borough and train station. The data visualization above uses data from the two reports on Q2 2026 to show the racial breakdown for summonses and arrests.
